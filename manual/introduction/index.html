Introduction

ASM Lab 1.45¶

ASM Lab is a mine ventilation program for designing, checking and simulating the ventilation of underground mines. You draw the mine's airways in 3D, or import them from a drawing or another program, place the fans, regulators, walls and doors, and ASM Lab calculates the airflow in every airway. It then goes further than airflow: the temperature and humidity of the air, the gases and dust it carries, and how a fire would spread smoke and heat through the mine.

Version 1.45 is the release this manual describes. It brings together everything built into ASM Lab so far: the network solver, the thermal solver, the fire module, the duct module, scripting, the ASM Assistant, the human (worker) module and the engineering calculators.

What ASM Lab simulates¶

SimulationWhat you get
AirflowThe flow (m³/s), velocity, pressure drop and air power of every airway, the pressure at every junction, and the operating point of every fan.
Temperature and humidityDry- and wet-bulb temperature, relative humidity, moisture and heat content along every airway, with heat from the rock, machines, people, blasting and auto-compression, and cooling by bulk air coolers.
GasesCarbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, methane and other gases released in an airway, followed through the network against their exposure limits.
DustRespirable and inhalable dust and diesel particulate matter carried with the air.
FireHeat and smoke carried through the network over time: temperature, oxygen, CO and CO₂ at every place, airflow changes and reversals caused by the fire, and when the smoke first reaches each place.
PeopleHeat stress for workers at a stated work rate and clothing, and the human module for stope layouts and evacuation.

Mining methods¶

ASM Lab models any mine whose ventilation can be described as a network of airways joined at junctions, so it is not limited to one mining method.

  • Block and panel caving: extraction-level drawpoint drives, undercut levels, ore passes and crusher stations, on several levels joined at the shafts.
  • Bord and pillar: a grid of bords and pillars with walls, brattices and doors between them; the standard wall and door types are included.
  • Narrow-reef stoping: inclined stopes between strike drives, with raises and the return airways to the upcast shaft.
  • Sublevel and long-hole stoping, and decline mines: ramps, sublevels and stopes, with auxiliary fans and ducts to the headings.
  • Coal seams: several seams in one model, with their own levels and connections.

What ASM Lab is for¶

  • Planning and design: sizing airways and fans for a new mine or an extension, testing changes before they are built, and comparing options.
  • Checking an operating mine: building the model from survey data, calibrating it against measured airflows, and finding where the air is short or the temperature too high.
  • Emergency preparedness: fire scenarios, escape routes and refuge bays.
  • Monitoring (planned): a digital twin of the mine, fed by sensors and machine-learning models, so the model follows the mine as it changes.

How ASM Lab works¶

ASM Lab describes the mine as a network. Each excavation the air passes through (a drive, a shaft, a raise, a stope) is an airway, drawn as its centre line in 3D with a cross-section. Airways meet at nodes: a node where airways meet is a junction, and a node at the end of an airway is an end, open to the surface or closed.

Each airway has a resistance to airflow, worked out from its length, its cross-section and the roughness of its walls (the Atkinson friction factor k), plus any shock losses at bends and changes of section. Devices placed on airways add or remove pressure: fans push the air, regulators and walls restrict it. Boundaries tell ASM Lab where air enters and leaves the mine.

When you solve, ASM Lab finds the airflow in every airway so that the air entering each junction equals the air leaving it, and the pressure drops around every loop add up to what the fans supply. The thermal solver then follows the air through the network and works out how its temperature and moisture change in each airway.

Airway shapes¶

ShapeTypical use
RectangularDevelopment drives and crosscuts cut to a square profile.
Horseshoe and D-shapeDrives with an arched roof.
CircularShafts, raise-bored raises and ducts.
Elliptical and egg-shapedShafts and airways with a non-circular rounded section.
TrapezoidalWider at the floor than the roof, as in some drives and coal roadways.
CustomAny section you draw or import; its area and perimeter set the resistance.

Airway kinds¶

Every airway has a kind, which sets its default section and colour and lets you find and report airways by what they are: drive, shaft, station, ramp, raise, stope, drawpoint, ore pass and chamber.

Devices and boundaries¶

ItemWhat it does
Fan (main, booster, auxiliary)Adds pressure to its airway along a fan curve (pressure, efficiency and power against flow), at a set pressure, or holding a set flow.
RegulatorRestricts an airway to hold a set flow, a set pressure drop, a set resistance or a set opening.
Wall, door, seal, brattice, louvreCloses or restricts an airway; standard types with their resistances are included (brick, concrete, shotcrete, good and poor seals, brattice, louvre, rope choke and others).
CoolerCools and dries the air in an airway to a set outlet state or duty.
Refuge bayMarks a refuge chamber on an airway, for fire and escape studies.
Intake and exhaustBoundaries where air enters or leaves the mine at a given pressure and temperature.

How it calculates¶

  • Airflow: the square law of resistance (pressure drop = R × Q²) with Atkinson's friction factor, corrected for air density, solved for the whole network at once by Newton's method; fans follow their curves, corrected for speed and air density by the fan laws.
  • Thermal: the air is followed airway by airway, with heat from the rock (from the virgin rock temperature, the age of the airway and the wetness of its walls), auto-compression in shafts, machines, people, blasting and coolers; airflow and air density are solved again until both settle.
  • Psychrometry: standard psychrometric equations for mine air at the pressure at each point, the same equations as the calculators.
  • Fire: smoke and heat carried along the airways as the fire grows, with heat lost to the rock walls, and buoyancy and throttling changing the airflow as the fire burns.
  • Stopes and ducts: detailed 2D and 3D flow solvers for a single stope and for duct systems.

The equations, their sources and the checks against hand calculations and other programs are in the Solver guide.

Ways to build a model¶

  • Draw in 3D: click points in the 3D view to draw airways, shafts and curves, then place devices on them.
  • Import: DXF drawings, JSON network files, spreadsheets of nodes and airways, Datamine files and 3D models.
  • Ask the ASM Assistant: describe what you want in plain words, for example "draw a ramp with two stopes and solve it", and the assistant draws and solves it, then explains the results.
  • Script it: write short JavaScript scripts that draw, change and solve the model, using the functions listed in the Script panel. Python scripting is planned for a later version and will need Python installed on the computer.

Document conventions¶

Typographical conventions¶

FormatMeaning
BoldA button, menu, tab, field or other part of the screen, a file name, or text you type. For example: click Solve.
ItalicEmphasis, or a term explained where it first appears.
<angle brackets>Text that changes with each use, such as <project name>. Do not type the brackets.

Keyboard conventions¶

Key combinationMeaning
key+keyHold down the first key and press the second. For example, Ctrl+S means hold Ctrl and press S.
A single keyPress it with the 3D view active. For example, A starts drawing a tunnel.

Menu conventions¶

File > Save As means click the File menu, then Save As. Home > Create > Draw tunnel means the Home tab of the ribbon, the Create group, the Draw tunnel button.

Mouse conventions¶

ActionDescription
ClickPress and release the left mouse button.
Right-clickPress and release the right mouse button. While drawing, right-click finishes the airway chain.
Double-clickClick the left button twice quickly.
Left-drag in the 3D viewRotates the view.
Right-drag in the 3D viewPans the view.
WheelZooms the view in and out.
