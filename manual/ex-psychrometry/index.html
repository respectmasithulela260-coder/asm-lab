Worked examples: psychrometry and heat

The questions come from the MINN3019A mine ventilation course (tutorials, class calculations, self-study problems and lecture slides). Each one is set out as the question, the hand solution as in the memo, and then the same question answered with ASM Lab, with the difference from the memo. Memo values read off psychrometric charts carry the chart's reading accuracy, about 0.3 kJ/kg on sigma heat and 0.2 °C on a temperature, so differences of that size are reading differences, not errors.

Mixing return air with intake air¶

Tutorial 2, question 1.

The air entering a stope must not have a wet bulb above 29 °C. 20 m³/s of return air is available at 32 °C saturated, and intake air at 26.7/32.2 °C. The barometric pressure is 100 kPa. How much intake air must be mixed with the return air?

Hand solution¶

From the 100 kPa chart: return air sigma heat 107.2 kJ/kg and specific volume 0.92 m³/kg; intake air 81.5 kJ/kg and 0.906 m³/kg; the mixture at 29 °C wet bulb 92.2 kJ/kg.

m_return = 20 / 0.92 = 21.75 kg/s
A × 81.5 + 21.75 × 107.2 = (A + 21.75) × 92.2, so A = 30.4 kg/s
Q = 30.4 × 0.906 = 27.5 m³/s
Answer: 27.5 m³/s of intake air.

With ASM Lab¶

Task: Find the intake quantity with the mixing calculator

  1. Open Tools > Calculators > Air mixing at 100 kPa. Stream 1: 32/32 °C, 20 m³/s. Stream 2: 26.7/32.2 °C.
  2. Change the quantity of stream 2 until the mixture's wet bulb is 29.0 °C: 29.0 m³/s.

Pump chamber air: to return, or back into the intake?¶

Tutorial 2, question 2.

75 m³/s of intake air at 18/26 °C passes a pump chamber; 13 m³/s of it ventilates the chamber, where 70 % of the pumps' 1 000 kW goes into the air. The return wet bulb from the section must not exceed 32 °C, and the barometric pressure is 100 kPa. Scheme 1 sends the pump chamber air straight to return; scheme 2 lets it rejoin the intake. What is the heat absorption capacity of the air for the section in each scheme, and which is better?

Hand solution¶

From the 100 kPa chart: intake air 50.2 kJ/kg and 0.873 m³/kg; return at 32 °C saturated 107.2 kJ/kg.

Scheme 1: m = 62 / 0.873 = 71 kg/s; capacity = 71 × (107.2 − 50.2) = 4 050 kW
Scheme 2: m = 75 / 0.873 = 85.9 kg/s; capacity = 85.9 × 57 − 700 = 4 200 kW
Answer: scheme 1 4 050 kW, scheme 2 4 200 kW; scheme 2 is better by about 150 kW.

With ASM Lab¶

Task: Compare the schemes with the psychrometric calculator

  1. In Tools > Calculators > Psychrometry at 100 kPa, enter 26/18 °C and then 32/32 °C and read the sigma heat and specific volume.
  2. Multiply the mass flows by the difference in sigma heat, and take off the 700 kW of the pumps in scheme 2.

A deep downcast shaft and a haulage¶

Tutorial 3.

300 m³/s of air at 13.6/20.0 °C enters an 1 800 m deep downcast shaft at 84.7 kPa. In the shaft the air picks up 1.15 kJ/kg per 300 m of heat and 0.5 g/kg per 200 m of moisture from the surroundings; auto-compression adds 9.79 kJ/kg per 1 000 m. At the bottom (105 kPa) it travels 1 600 m along a haulage, picking up 2 890 kW of heat and 1.8 l/s of water. Find the condition of the air at the end of the haulage and the share of the shaft's heat that comes from auto-compression.

Hand solution¶

At the shaft top (85 kPa chart): r = 9.0 g/kg, S = 42.4 kJ/kg, mass flow 297.6 kg/s.

Auto-compression: 9.79 × 1.8 = 17.62 kJ/kg; heat from the rock: 1.15 × 1 800 / 300 = 6.9 kJ/kg
Moisture in the shaft: 0.5 × 1 800 / 200 = 4.5 g/kg
Haulage: 2 890 / 297.6 = 9.71 kJ/kg and 1 800 / 297.6 = 6.05 g/kg
End of haulage: S = 42.4 + 17.62 + 6.9 + 9.71 = 76.63 kJ/kg, r = 9.0 + 4.5 + 6.05 = 19.55 g/kg
From the 105 kPa chart: 26.1/28.8 °C
Auto-compression: 297.6 × 17.62 = 5 244 kW, which is 17.62 / 24.52 = 71.9 % of the shaft's heat
Answer: 26.1/28.8 °C at the end of the haulage; auto-compression 5 244 kW, 71.9 % of the heat added in the shaft.

With ASM Lab¶

Task: Follow the air down the shaft

  1. In Tools > Calculators > Psychrometry enter 84.7 kPa and 20/13.6 °C for the moisture, sigma heat and specific volume at the top.
  2. Add the heat and moisture as above, then find the state at 105 kPa with that sigma heat and moisture: ASM Lab gives 26.1/28.7 °C.

Heat from pumps and rock in a crosscut¶

Class calculations 1, question 2.

55.3 m³/s of air at 29.1/35.3 °C enters 56 level crosscut at A (117.5 kPa). Part of it ventilates a pump chamber with four 1 500 kW pumps whose motors are 94 % efficient, and then rejoins the main stream at B. At the end of the crosscut, C, the air is at 31.1/34.8 °C. Find (a) the temperature at B, assuming no moisture is added, and (b) the heat from the rock and the moisture picked up between A and C.

Hand solution¶

From the 117.5 kPa chart at A: S = 82.8 kJ/kg, r = 19.3 g/kg, 0.778 m³/kg, so m = 55.3 / 0.778 = 71.1 kg/s.

Pump heat: 4 × (1 500 / 0.94 − 1 500) = 383 kW, so ΔS = 383 / 71.1 = 5.4 kJ/kg
S_B = 82.8 + 5.4 = 88.2 kJ/kg at r = 19.3 g/kg: 30.3/40.3 °C
At C: S = 91.5 kJ/kg, r = 23.3 g/kg; heat A to C = 71.1 × 8.7 = 618.6 kW
Heat from the rock: 618.6 − 383 = 236 kW; water: 71.1 × (23.3 − 19.3) = 284 g/s = 0.284 l/s
Answer: (a) 30.3/40.3 °C at B; (b) 236 kW from the rock and 0.284 l/s of water.
