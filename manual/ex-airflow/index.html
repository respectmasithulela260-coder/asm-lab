Worked examples: airflow, pressure and regulators

The questions come from the MINN3019A mine ventilation course (tutorials, class calculations, self-study problems and lecture slides). Each one is set out as the question, the hand solution as in the memo, and then the same question answered with ASM Lab, with the difference from the memo. Memo values read off psychrometric charts carry the chart's reading accuracy, about 0.3 kJ/kg on sigma heat and 0.2 °C on a temperature, so differences of that size are reading differences, not errors.

Pitot tube in an air pipe¶

Tutorial 1, question 2.

A pitot tube is installed at the midpoint of a 250 mm air pipe. The velocity pressure measured on a manometer is 8 mm water and the static pressure 6 mm water. The air temperature is 21 °C and the barometric pressure 744 mm mercury. The flow is turbulent and the average velocity is 82 % of the velocity at the centre of the pipe. Calculate the volume flow and the mass flow of the air. Take g = 9.79 m/s², mercury 13 550 kg/m³ and water 1 000 kg/m³, and treat air as an ideal gas.

Hand solution¶

The barometric pressure and the density of (dry) air:

p = ρ_Hg g h = 13 550 × 9.79 × 0.744 = 98 692 Pa
ρ = p / (R T) = 98 692 / (287 × 294.15) = 1.17 kg/m³

The velocity pressure and the centre-line velocity:

VP = ρ_w g h = 1 000 × 9.79 × 0.008 = 78.3 Pa
u = √(2 VP / ρ) = √(2 × 78.3 / 1.17) = 11.6 m/s

The average velocity, the volume flow and the mass flow:

u_avg = 0.82 × 11.6 = 9.5 m/s
Q = u_avg × π D² / 4 = 9.5 × 0.049 = 0.465 m³/s
m = ρ Q = 1.17 × 0.5 = 0.585 kg/s (the memo rounds Q to 0.5 first)
Answer: Q = 0.465 m³/s (say 0.5 m³/s); m = 0.585 kg/s in the memo.

With ASM Lab¶

Task: Answer it with the calculators

  1. Open Tools > Calculators and choose Psychrometry. Enter 98.69 kPa, a dry bulb of 21 °C and a relative humidity of 0 %. The dry-air density is 1.169 kg/m³.
  2. Choose Velocity pressure. Enter the density and change the velocity until the velocity pressure reads 78.3 Pa: 11.58 m/s.
  3. The rest is the area and the 0.82 factor: Q = 0.466 m³/s, m = 0.545 kg/s.

Two crosscuts in parallel: doors, regulator and booster fan¶

Class calculations 1, question 1.

Two crosscuts lead from a sub-vertical shaft to a stoping section. The upper crosscut AD is 3.0 m × 3.0 m and 400 m long. All the air, 80 m³/s, now flows in the lower crosscut ABCD, and the pressure across the doors in the upper crosscut is 1.05 kPa. The air density is 1.25 kg/m³.

  1. What quantity flows in each crosscut if the doors are left open?
  2. What size of regulator in the upper crosscut sends 60 m³/s through ABCD?
  3. Keeping the pressure available unchanged, what booster fan in the lower crosscut keeps 20 m³/s in the upper one?

Hand solution¶

The resistance of ABCD at the prevailing density, from the door pressure, and of AD from its size with an assumed k-factor of 0.018 Ns²/m⁴:

R_ABCD = p / Q² = 1 050 / 80² = 0.1641 Ns²/m⁸
R_AD = k C L / A³ = 0.018 × 12 × 400 / 9³ = 0.11852 Ns²/m⁸

(a) With the doors open the two are in parallel:

1/√R_T = 1/√0.11852 + 1/√0.1641, so R_T = 0.03464 Ns²/m⁸
p = R_T Q² = 0.03464 × 80² = 221.7 Pa
Q_AD = √(p / R_AD) = √(221.7 / 0.11852) = 43.25 m³/s; Q_ABCD = 80 − 43.25 = 36.75 m³/s

(b) For 60 m³/s in ABCD the circuit needs R_ABCD × 60² = 591 Pa, while 20 m³/s in AD uses only R_AD × 20² = 47.4 Pa. The regulator takes the difference, 543.6 Pa:

A = 1.2 Q √(ρ / p) = 1.2 × 20 × √(1.25 / 543.6) = 1.15 m²

(c) Keeping 221.7 Pa across the circuit, the regulator now takes 221.7 − 47.4 = 174.3 Pa (A = 2.03 m²) and a booster in ABCD supplies the rest of the 591 Pa:

p_booster = 591 − 221.7 = 369.3 Pa at 60 m³/s
Answer: (a) 43.3 and 36.8 m³/s; (b) a 1.2 m² regulator; (c) a 2 m² regulator and a booster of 60 m³/s at 369 Pa.

With ASM Lab¶

Task: Build the two crosscuts as a network

  1. Draw the two crosscuts between the same two nodes and give them the resistances above in the Inspect panel (Resistance override).
  2. (a) Put a fan set to Fixed quantity, 80 m³/s, on the shaft airway and press F5. The solver splits the air: 43.24 m³/s in AD and 36.76 m³/s in ABCD, at 221.6 Pa.
  3. (b) Add a regulator to AD in target pressure drop mode with 543.6 Pa. ASM Lab sizes it at 1.151 m². Set that area, solve again, and ABCD carries 60.00 m³/s.
  4. (c) Set the shaft to a fixed pressure difference of 221.7 Pa, give AD a regulator of 2.03 m² and put a Fixed quantity booster of 60 m³/s in ABCD. The solver reports the booster pressure, 369.0 Pa, with 20.00 m³/s left in AD.
